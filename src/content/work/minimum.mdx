<Section title="Simple, Not Lightweight">
<Column>

Carbon accounting is complicated. Minimum pulls emissions data from across a business, then works out what it all adds up to, auditable line by line. The people buying it were large corporations, alongside startups managing their own footprint.

</Column>
<Column>

The brand had to make that feel simple without making it look lightweight. Precise enough for a compliance team, clear enough for everyone else.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 812]} src={socialGrid} rect={[0, -66, 1416, 944]} alt="A grid of Minimum social posts: big monospaced emissions figures like “453 tCO2e” and “000”, “Building for net-zero” and a list of emission sources with “Waste” highlighted in blue" />

<Section title="Nine Marks, Two Routes">
<Column>

It started with a brand workshop with **Freddie Evans**, Minimum’s CEO, and **Chris Winchurch**, their CPTO. Out of it I drew **nine marks**, from slanted bars and a chart line to an m with a minus sign and a stack of six dots.

</Column>
<Column>

Two became routes. The first leaned on the science, with six dots for carbon, set in Space Grotesk with a blueberry blue and a pop of acid green. The second was the Minus M, set in Alliance No.1 with a cobalt blue we picked from Chris’s coat.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="A Rule of Six">
<Column>

The final brand brought the two routes together: the six particles from the first, with the type and blue from the second. Carbon’s atomic number is **six**.

</Column>
<Column>

The particles sit a sixth of their size apart, and the mark sits one particle’s width from the wordmark. The same particles set the clear space: four of them around the full logo, one around the mark.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 812]} src={logoConstruction} rect={[0, 0, 1416, 812]} alt="The Minimum logo layout from the guidelines: six particles for the protons, neutrons and electrons in a carbon atom, spaced a sixth of their size apart, with the wordmark a particle’s width away and a stem’s width between letters" />

<Section title="Sharp Angles, Fixed Widths">
<Column>

The logotype is a customised Alliance No.1 from Degarism. I chose it for its sharp angles and ink traps, which read as precise without feeling cold. For the wordmark I refined those ink traps, and a few of the letterforms with them.

</Column>
<Column>

Alliance also has a monospaced cut. Every digit takes the same width, so emissions figures line up like a ledger: 453.6 tCO₂e, 0.00 net-zero.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="One Accent at a Time">
<Column>

Minimum’s blue is the constant, bold enough to pick a number out on black or on white. Around it sit five bright accents, each with its own gradient.

</Column>
<Column>

The rule is one accent per page. Flat colour for small details, gradients for big areas, and only pink and violet on light backgrounds.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Only Ever a Crop">
<Column>

The patterns are spirals, built by rotating one shape around a centre point, step by step, until it closes. For a fading spiral, each step drops the contrast by the same amount.

</Column>
<Column>

In use, we only ever show a crop. It hints at the data behind every number: there’s always more there than you can see.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="In the Guide">
<Column>

All of it went into a **21-page guide** covering the logo, type, colour, patterns and icons, alongside new messaging.

</Column>
<Column>

The grid has four columns and stretches from a square post to a 16:9 slide, so a social post and the website’s header follow the same rules.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="What happened next" glow>

<Section title="What Happened Next">
<Column>

Minimum ran on the brand for **over three years**. In that time it partnered with Novisto, a sustainability platform based in Montréal, and the two came to share enterprise customers.

</Column>
<Column>

**Outcome:**

In March 2026 Novisto acquired Minimum. Its carbon accounting, the thing the brand was built to make feel simple, now runs inside Novisto’s platform.

</Column>

<Section title="Counting the Invisible">
<Column>

Minimum’s product is complicated. It pulls emissions data from across a business, then works out what it all adds up to, auditable line by line. The people buying it were large corporations, alongside startups managing their own footprint.

</Column>
<Column>

The brand had to make that feel simple without making it look lightweight. Precise enough for a compliance team, clear enough for everyone else.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 944]} src={socialGrid} rect={[0, 0, 1416, 944]} alt="A grid of Minimum social posts: big monospaced emissions figures like “453 tCO2e” and “000”, “Building for net-zero” and a list of emission sources with “Waste” highlighted in blue" />

<Section title="Two Routes">
<Column>

The first route came out of the science. A carbon atom has six protons, six neutrons and six electrons, so I drew a logomark from six dots and set it in Space Grotesk, with a blueberry blue and a pop of acid green.

</Column>
<Column>

Minimum liked the science but not the type or the colours, and asked to try an “m” mark. So the second route was a Minus M: an m with a short dash, for minimising your footprint, set in Alliance No.1 with a cobalt blue.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="A Rule of Six">
<Column>

The final brand took the six particles from the first route and the type and blue from the second. Minimum’s whole business is counting things you can’t see, and six gave us something to count.

</Column>
<Column>

The particles stack like an abacus, a sixth of their size apart, and the mark sits a quarter of a particle above the baseline. The same particles set the logo’s clear space: four of them around the full logo, one around the mark.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 708]} src={logoGrid} rect={[0, -118, 1416, 944]} alt="The Minimum logo in white on blue, on its construction grid, with faded particles marking the clear space" />

<Section title="Sharp Angles, Fixed Widths">
<Column>

The logotype is a customised Alliance No.1 from Degarism. I chose it for its sharp angles and ink traps, which read as precise without feeling cold, then refined the cuts where each stem meets its shoulder.

</Column>
<Column>

Alliance also has a monospaced cut. Every digit takes the same width, so emissions figures line up like a ledger: 453.6 tCO₂e, 0.00 net-zero.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="One Accent at a Time">
<Column>

Minimum’s blue is the constant, bold enough to pick a number out on black or on white. Around it sit five bright accents, each with its own gradient.

</Column>
<Column>

The rule is one accent per page. Flat colour for small details, gradients for big areas, and only pink and violet on light backgrounds.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Only Ever a Crop">
<Column>

The patterns are spirals, built by rotating one shape around a centre point, step by step, until it closes. For a fading spiral, each step drops the contrast by the same amount.

</Column>
<Column>

We only ever show a crop. It hints at the data behind every number: there’s always more there than you can see.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="In the Guide">
<Column>

All of it went into a 21-page guide covering the logo, type, colour, patterns, icons and a four-column grid that stretches from a square post to a 16:9 slide, alongside new messaging.

</Column>
<Column>

**Outcome:**

Minimum kept the brand right up until Novisto acquired the company.

</Column>

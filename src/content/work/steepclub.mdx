<CaseIntro title="Packaging that tells the time">
  <Fragment slot="lead">In autumn 2022 I branded The Steep Club, a tea subscription sold direct to customers, for its founder **Heather Melcer**. She wanted a tea brand for Millennials and Gen Z that looked nothing like the ones already out there. There were two ranges, Rise to wake you up and Unwind to settle you down. Could they look like one brand and still be told apart at a glance?</Fragment>

<Section title="Steeped, Not Brewed">
<Column>

It turns out tea isn’t brewed, as I’d always thought. It’s steeped. Brewing keeps the water on the heat. Steeping takes it off and lets the leaves soak until their flavour comes out.

</Column>
<Column>

So the brand draws out what’s inside too. Each range leads with one bold colour that tells you what’s in the box before you’ve read a word, so you know whether you’re reaching for morning or night.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 812]} src={packsFlat} rect={[-127.5, -151, 1671, 1114]} alt="The Unwind and Rise packs laid flat on cream: a black grid over a gradient, pale lilac deepening to violet for Unwind, pale yellow deepening to acid yellow for Rise, each with the range and the tea’s name in a black tab up the side" />

<Section title="Midnight and Energise">
<Column>

The ranges didn’t start as Rise and Unwind. My first route called them Midnight and Energise, ran the name up the pack in the same heavy capitals, and gave them plum and orange.

</Column>
<Column>

The heavy capitals stayed. The names and the colours didn’t: by the final round they were Rise and Unwind, in acid yellow and purple.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Rise and Unwind">
<Column>

The two ranges do opposite jobs. They had to be different enough to tell apart, and close enough to read as one club. So I gave both the same pack: a grid with a gradient running down it. Together they stand for time passing, which is the one thing that separates a morning tea from a night-time one.

</Column>
<Column>

The colour is the difference. Rise’s gradient runs to acid yellow, a nod to the morning sun, and Unwind’s to purple, for the night drawing in. Everything else stays the same, down to the black tab the tea’s name sits in.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Join the Partea">
<Column>

The type is Thunder, a heavy condensed sans set in capitals, big enough to fill the side of a box. On the shipping boxes, “Join the Partea” repeats until it runs off every edge. On the packs it turns on its side.

</Column>
<Column>

I wrote the rest of the copy as the same kind of joke: “Smells like tea spirit” on one poster, “Let the loose-leaf tea loose” on another, and “Brew can do it” along the packing tape.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Brew Can Do It">
<Column>

The monthly box belongs to neither range: plain kraft card, “Your monthly tea subscription” in black, and the “Brew can do it” tape across the top in green, the one colour that isn’t Rise’s or Unwind’s.

</Column>
<Column>

As far as I know, The Steep Club never launched, so the packs only ever lived in mockups like these.

</Column>

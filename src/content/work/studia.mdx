<Chapter label="The story">

<Section title="Where It Started">
<Column>

When I joined, Studia already had a product: livestream replays in a Video Vault, homework after each lesson, and an AI chat called Sigma. Parents got one card per child.

</Column>
<Column>

**Change:**

I kept what worked, the live lessons, replays and homework, and redesigned the rest: a calmer style, and separate experiences for tutors, students and parents.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 812]} src={before} rect={[0, 0, 1416, 812]} caption="Studia when I joined, September 2025" alt="Six screens of Studia when I joined: the Video Vault of orange gradient livestream thumbnails, a student home of homework cards in bright subject pills, Sigma the AI chat, and homework questions" />

<Section title="Three Customers">
<Column>

Most tutoring platforms are built for one customer. Studia had three. Tutors wanted more than an hourly rate, from platforms that took 25–40% of it. Students wanted to know what to revise tonight. Parents were paying, and wanted proof it was working.

</Column>
<Column>

Each of them measured success differently, so it couldn’t be one product with three logins. Our interviews with students made the point sharper: most weren’t short of content, they were short of a plan.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 508]} src={threeCustomers} rect={[0, 0, 1416, 508]} caption="Three customers, three ideas of success" alt="Three illustrations, one per customer. Tutor: a five-star tutor ringed by a clock and a money bag. Student: a girl revising alone, a sad face beside her. Parent: two tutor cards with their hourly prices" />

</Chapter>

<Chapter label="The brand" glow>

<Section title="Two Routes">
<Column>

I explored two directions for the brand. *Assured Academic* led with trust: a scholastic serif, grounded colours and gentle, page-turning motion. *Creator Live* led with energy: loud display type, punchy colour and live reactions.

</Column>
<Column>

**Outcome:**

We kept something from each. The brand took Creator Live’s condensed wordmark and neon. The product took Assured Academic’s calm, because parents had to trust it with their children’s education.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="Tutors" glow>

<Section title="Earning a Place">
<Column>

Studia had 3,500 tutor applications, and every tutor would be teaching children. So onboarding wasn’t a form. It was a vetting process: profile, review, a 15-minute interview, an enhanced DBS check, then your first lesson.

</Column>
<Column>

I made the dashboard the onboarding. Schedule, Performance and Inbox stay locked until you’re through, and each step explains itself: rates are suggested by years of experience, from £30–45 an hour to £60–150, and payouts go through Stripe Connect.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 1416]} src={earningAPlace} rect={[0, 0, 1416, 1416]} alt="Onboarding components: a set-up checklist, “Book your interview!” with the five steps, locked Schedule, Performance and Messages cards, connecting a bank account, suggested hourly rates by experience, payout alerts, card details and subscriptions" />

<Section title="A Profile, Not a Listing">
<Column>

Tutoring sites make tutors look like listings: a photo, a price and a list of subjects. Portfolio sites like Contra and Read.cv make people look like people. Studia’s tutors needed both, credentials for the parent and personality for the student.

</Column>
<Column>

It took more than 50 versions to get there: from a wireframe that read like a listing to a page that opens with a line in the tutor’s own voice, then the proof a parent looks for. DBS, verified ID, reviews, grades improved and how fast they reply.

</Column>
</Section>

<Column>

One template had to cover two kinds of tutor. Hero tutors, the handful Studia planned to launch with, teach live and sell a monthly subscription. Everyone else teaches one to one.

</Column>
<Column>

**Change:**

Hero profiles get a library of past livestreams and a Subscribe to Live card. Everyone else gets a free introduction call instead, which is what a new tutor needs most.

</Column>

<Section title="Earning the Mic">
<Column>

The first analytics wireframe was a wall of numbers. I asked a different question: what should a tutor do differently tomorrow? Each insight came with one button: open Thursday afternoon, add a midpoint poll, follow up with Sarah.

</Column>
<Column>

We put it in front of five Studia tutors for 45 minutes each, and cut by what they did. The weekly calendar and income breakdown stayed. Time utilisation, measured against a maximum nobody had chosen, went. No-shows came out of the headline numbers. AI insights stayed, but each one got a “Why?” first.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 812]} src={tutorUserTests} rect={[0, 0, 1416, 812]} caption="The modules five tutors tested" alt="The modules tutors tested: time utilisation, AI insights, unlocking livestreams, revenue breakdown and KPIs" />

<Section>
<Column>

I worked most closely with **Ben Lavender**, Studia’s CPO, who’d worked on streaming at BBC iPlayer, LOVEFiLM and DAZN. Livestreams were the big promise, so we made them something you earn: 50 lessons, a 90% attendance rate, a 4.5 rating, three subjects and a verified ID.

</Column>
<Column>

**Change:**

Students only see tutors live once they’ve proved themselves one to one, and new tutors can see exactly what it takes to get there.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="The Teaching Assistant">
<Column>

Studia AI worked inside the lesson rather than beside it. When a student asked a question in the chat, it could answer straight away, so the tutor didn’t have to stop teaching.

</Column>
<Column>

It also read the tutor’s lesson plan and suggested quick-fire polls, timed to the minute. Nothing went out until the tutor pressed Add Question.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="Students and parents" glow>

<Section title="Who Pays, Who Learns">
<Column>

Students had told us they were short of a plan, so Oliver’s dashboard leads with what to do next: his next class and a Join button. The first draft, out of Figma Make, put everything on one screen. Three rounds later, spend and subscriptions had moved to his parent.

</Column>
<Column>

Sarah’s dashboard leads with what each of her children did this month, and what it cost. When a student subscribes to a tutor, the parent approves it. Under-16s can’t pay for anything themselves, so billing lives with the parent.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="The system" glow>

<Section title="The System">
<Column>

Underneath it all is a small design system, built in Figma alongside the screens. Baikal for type, calm neutrals, and one neon green saved for Hero tutors and the things you can do, like Subscribe, Create and Go.

</Column>
<Column>

Six softer accents mark subjects, session types and badges. From those atoms came the pieces the whole product is built from: tutor cards, booking cards, class cards, notifications and reviews.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="What happened next" glow>

<Section title="What Happened Next">
<Column>

Studia is live at [studia.io](https://studia.io). I wrapped up in January 2026 and handed over a design system, the tutor journey from application to first livestream, and the student and parent dashboards to build on.

</Column>
<Column>

**Looking back:**

The exciting idea was a creator platform for tutors. But parents only buy into that if the basics feel safe: vetting, honest profiles, and a dashboard that tells each person what to do next. That’s where most of my time went, and I’d start there again.

</Column>

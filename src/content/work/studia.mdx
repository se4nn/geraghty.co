<Chapter label="The story">

<StudiaOpener alt="The STUDIA wordmark in black on neon, “Redefining tutoring for the digital age” beneath it, with a tutor card for Eleasha Richardson, a “The best tutor ever” quote, a fire emoji and a cursor named Sophia floating around it" />

<Section title="Who Pays, Who Learns">
<Column>

Most tutoring platforms are built for one customer. Studia had three. Tutors’ income was capped by the clock, with platforms taking **25–40%** of it. Students’ learning was split across lessons, apps and videos, with nothing joining it up. Parents were paying, and wanted proof it was working.

</Column>
<Column>

So it couldn’t be one product with three logins. The first draft, out of Figma Make, put everything on one screen. Three rounds later, Oliver’s dashboard led with his next class and a Join button, and spend had moved to his parent, Sarah. She approves every subscription, because under-16s can’t pay for anything themselves.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="The brand" glow>

<Section title="Two Routes">
<Column>

I designed the pitch deck for the raise, and explored two routes for the brand. *Assured Academic* led with trust: a scholastic serif and grounded colours. *Creator Live* led with energy: loud display type, punchy colour and live reactions.

</Column>
<Column>

**Outcome:**

We kept something from each. The brand took Creator Live’s condensed wordmark and neon. The product took Assured Academic’s calm, because parents had to trust it with their children’s education.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="Tutors" glow>

<Section title="Earning a Place">
<Column>

Studia had **3,500 tutor applications**, and every tutor would be teaching children. So onboarding wasn’t a form. It was a vetting process: profile, review, a 15-minute interview, an enhanced DBS check, then your first lesson.

</Column>
<Column>

I made the dashboard the onboarding. Schedule, Performance and Inbox stay locked until you’re through, and each step explains itself, from suggested rates by experience to payouts through Stripe Connect.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 1416]} src={earningAPlace} rect={[0, 0, 1416, 1416]} alt="Onboarding components: a set-up checklist, “Book your interview!” with the five steps, locked Schedule, Performance and Messages cards, connecting a bank account, suggested hourly rates by experience, payout alerts, card details and subscriptions" />

<Section title="A Profile, Not a Listing">
<Column>

Tutoring sites make tutors look like listings: a photo, a price and a list of subjects. Portfolio sites like Contra and Read.cv make people look like people. Studia’s tutors needed both, credentials for the parent and personality for the student.

</Column>
<Column>

It took **more than 50 versions**, from a wireframe that read like a listing to a page that opens in the tutor’s own voice, then shows the proof a parent looks for. Hero tutors, the handful Studia planned to launch with, add past livestreams and a Subscribe card. Everyone else gets a free introduction call, which is what a new tutor needs most.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Marking Our Homework">
<Column>

Before building the tutor dashboard, we put it in front of **five Studia tutors** for 45 minutes each. Some only taught one to one, others livestreamed too. We wanted to know what they understood, what they’d act on and what they’d ignore.

</Column>
<Column>

**Outcome:**

The calendar and income breakdown were what they looked at first, so they stayed. Time utilisation was measured against the wrong thing: their maximum availability, not the hours they actually wanted to teach. No-shows came out of the headline numbers. AI insights stayed, as long as each one showed why.

</Column>
</Section>

<Crop window={[1416, 812]} src={tutorUserTests} rect={[0, 0, 1416, 812]} caption="The modules five tutors tested" alt="The modules tutors tested: time utilisation, AI insights, unlocking livestreams, revenue breakdown and KPIs" />

<Section title="Earning the Mic">
<Column>

I worked most closely with **Ben Lavender**, Studia’s CPO, who’d worked on streaming at BBC iPlayer, LOVEFiLM and DAZN. Livestreams were the big promise, so we made them something you earn: 50 lessons, a 90% attendance rate, a 4.5 rating, three subjects and a verified ID.

</Column>
<Column>

**Change:**

In testing, tutors read the criteria as boxes to tick. They became a progress meter instead, so a new tutor can see how far they are from going live, and students only see tutors live once they’ve proved themselves one to one.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="Studia AI" glow>

<Section title="The Teaching Assistant">
<Column>

Studia AI worked inside the lesson rather than beside it. When a student asked a question in the chat, it could answer straight away, so the tutor didn’t have to stop teaching.

</Column>
<Column>

It also read the tutor’s lesson plan and suggested quick-fire polls, timed to the minute. Nothing went out until the tutor pressed Add Question.

</Column>
</Section>

<Chapter label="What happened next" glow>

<Section title="What Happened Next">
<Column>

Studia is live at [studia.io](https://studia.io). I wrapped up in January 2026 and handed over the tutor journey from application to first livestream, the student and parent dashboards, and the small design system underneath: Baikal type, calm neutrals and one neon green saved for Hero tutors and the things you can do.

</Column>
<Column>

**Looking back:**

The exciting idea was a creator platform for tutors. But parents only buy into that if the basics feel safe: vetting, honest profiles, and a dashboard that tells each person what to do next. That’s where most of my time went, and I’d start there again.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Orders, However They Arrive">
<Column>

Reading PDFs from email was the right place to start: it’s how a lot of wholesale orders arrive. But makers also take orders by hand, online through Shopify (or other stores), over socials and even in store, and the next step was bringing those in too.

I mapped out what every order has in common (who it’s for, what’s in it, the price band, how many to a case), so an email became one way to process and track orders among several.

</Column>
<Column>

Some orders are clear and some aren’t. When the Order Hub isn’t sure, it asks: the email says “10x Orange”, so is that Bitter Orange, Mango & Citra Hops? The plan was for each answer to become a rule, so the same question doesn’t come up twice. When a PO’s price doesn’t match the last order, the maker can apply the new one to that shop or to every customer.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="A Library to Grow On">
<Column>

The new screens needed components the Order Hub didn’t have yet. The existing ones were MUI, styled a screen at a time, which got the team moving fast but meant more than 150 slightly different versions. With more screens on the way, I proposed moving to Base UI, shadcn/ui and Tailwind, with DELLI’s colours, spacing and type as tokens shared by Figma and code. It took under a week of groundwork before new screens could use it.

</Column>
<Column>

I built each component in code with help from Claude, working directly from its Figma spec. This is where my way of building components started: Claude learning from its own mistakes, reading the documentation before building, and checking earlier components for what we could reuse.

</Column>
</Section>

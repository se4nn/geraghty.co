<Section title="A Blank Slate">
<Column>

Fertifa came to me and my studio with a name and not much else. Their customers were founders, People teams and benefits leads at startups, tech companies and every kind of employer in between. The people actually using it were their teams.

</Column>
<Column>

That meant designing for two very different moments. Whatever I made had to be professional enough for a benefits review, and gentle enough for someone reading it alone, late at night.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Three Pillars">
<Column>

Over four or five rounds of workshops and feedback with **Eileen**, Fertifa’s CEO, and **Roxy**, their Head of Marketing, we worked out what Fertifa actually stood for. It came down to the three pillars above.

</Column>
<Column>

**Outcome:**

The pillars didn’t stay in a deck. “From start to finish and beyond” and “Supporting biological differences” went straight onto the posters.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Two Routes">
<Column>

I presented two directions. The first, *Facing reproductive health issues together*, put people’s own worries in the headline and set faces behind reeded glass. The second, *Supporting people through their reproductive healthcare challenges*, was softer: a leaf mark, lemon and sloe, and portraits in arches.

</Column>
<Column>

**Outcome:**

Fertifa chose the first, and I brought the warmth of the second along with it. The glass went after their feedback. It hinted there was something to hide, the opposite of what we wanted to say, so the faces came out from behind it.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="The Mark">
<Column>

The logomark is three ideas in one shape: the F of Fertifa, a + for health and a new shoot for life.

</Column>
<Column>

On its own it’s a stamp for app icons, letters and the corner of a poster. On the end of the wordmark it reads as a quiet plus.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="One Core Colour">
<Column>

In 2022 every startup was reaching for bright, loud colour (think Deliveroo and Monzo). A brand about something this personal needed to stay calm.

</Column>
<Column>

So one colour does the heavy lifting: a bold violet the team nicknamed “Blurple”. Around it sits a softer extended palette of greys, greens, yellows, blues and reds for backgrounds, illustration and the product, never all at once.

</Column>
</Section>

<Section title="Saying It Out Loud">
<Column>

The tone of voice says what people are actually thinking, in their words rather than ours: “I want to know more about IVF options.” “Reproductive challenges can be daunting, we’re here to change that.”

</Column>
<Column>

The illustrations do the rest. People drawn in a single purple line rather than stock photos of clinics, so the subject feels human without getting too close.

</Column>
</Section>
